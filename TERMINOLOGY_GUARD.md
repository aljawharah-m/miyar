# Terminology Guard — Scope and Provenance

## Why it exists
A translation may be fluent while replacing a precise Islamic term with a broader or different everyday word. Mi'yar therefore treats terminology precision as a separate safety layer.

## Three outcomes
1. **Accepted** — a clear equivalent is present.
2. **Risky** — the translation visibly flattens or changes the term; this can block publication.
3. **Context-dependent** — the equivalent can be right in some contexts but not all; Mi'yar requests review rather than claiming certainty.

## Example
- `الزكاة واجبة` → `Zakat is obligatory` — accepted by the local guard.
- `الزكاة واجبة` → `Charity is obligatory` — flagged because charity is broader and may be understood as optional giving, while zakat is a specific obligatory financial act with conditions, amounts and designated recipients.

## Provenance discipline
Rows supplied by the challenge scientific appendix are tagged separately as challenge glossary guidance. Expanded Mi'yar rows are tagged as conservative local engineering rules and are never presented as if they came from the official appendix. External recommended references remain separate from evidence actually used in a decision.

## Current expanded coverage
The local guard includes high-use concepts from creed, worship and fiqh, including: Islam, Tawhid, Sharia, worship, prophethood, revelation, Hadith, Sunnah, fatwa, da'wah, zakat, sadaqah, wudu, tayammum, fasting, Hajj, Umrah, riba, shirk, kufr, taqwa, dhikr, du'a, jihad, khamr/intoxicants, salah, niyyah, taharah, ghusl, qibla, adhan, ruku, sujud, ihram, kaffarah, waqf, iddah, hijab, awrah, nikah, mahr, iman, qadar, bid'ah, tawbah, istighfar and shaheed/martyr terminology.

The guard counts repeated occurrences. One correct occurrence does not mask a later flattened occurrence of the same term. It also avoids contaminating one correctly translated term with a different valid term elsewhere in the same sentence (for example, `Zakat ... charity` when `charity` correctly translates `الصدقة`). Explicit narrowing definitions such as `Sharia is criminal law` are still caught.

Coverage is intentionally extensible and not claimed to be exhaustive. Unknown or context-sensitive cases must be reviewed rather than guessed.
